# Tracker V4 research — Journal, Mood & Life

Research gathered 03/10/2026; synthesis completed 04/10/2026 (Asia/Bangkok). This is a research proposal, not an approved implementation specification. No production code changed. Public sources only; no private journal, photos or health records were uploaded. MASTER_PLAN.md was not read.

## Recommendation

Build a small daily capture flow around the existing journal, then make existing records easier to revisit. Prioritize selectable activity chips, a read-only **My Day** that joins local records by date, search, and weekly summaries with explicit coverage. Keep five familiar mood choices initially; offer optional feelings and energy separately. Delay automated correlations until missing-data semantics and mood measurement are clear.

DailyBean's useful lesson is how little a person must do before a day feels recorded: recognize a mood, tap familiar activities, optionally add a photo or words, then revisit a visual month. Tracker can apply that principle with its own paper surface, ink marks, Vietnamese wording and existing gym/nutrition records. Do not copy beans, character faces, theme illustrations, proprietary icons, wording or screen composition.

## Method and confidence

Used the Exa Search skill and its searching reference. **17 search queries, 88 requested result slots, 21 unique URLs fetched and read**, using capped text extraction. Requested slots are not 88 verified sources. Some slots were duplicates, two targeted Reddit searches returned no results, and a broader Daylio community query returned unrelated articles that were excluded. No app was installed, purchased or interactively tested. Storefront text can be stale or differ by country/version; the GB DailyBean listing was older than its US/Google Play listings.

Three passes: (1) official capability discovery for 11 named apps, (2) DailyBean and user-feedback gaps, (3) fetch the most relevant official pages and primary public reviews. The 21 fetched pages are enumerated below; other search highlights are marked where used. No claim of exhaustive global coverage, worldwide availability, market share or comparative popularity is made. These are meaningful international product references, chosen for different interaction models.

Evidence labels: **O** = official product/help/developer listing; **U** = an individual public user review, an anecdote rather than a representative survey; **S** = secondary source or search-only evidence; **P** = original Tracker proposal. Official marketing verifies advertised capabilities, not clinical effectiveness or independent security assurance.

## Existing Tracker baseline

Read DATA_MODEL.md, PHOTO_STORAGE.md, TONG_KET_DU_AN.md, js/journal.js, the journal/calendar/gallery sections of js/app.js, and js/photo-store.js. Schema 6 stores one optional journal entry per local date with stable ID, mood, title, note, tags and timestamps. Mood is optional; mood, text or a tag can make an entry valid. Current states are `drained/Mệt`, `low/Chùng`, `steady/Bình thường`, `good/Ổn`, `bright/Rất tốt`.

Activities are **partially implemented**: comma-separated custom tags, case-insensitive deduplication, and buttons for the first eight unique tags encountered in history. A button adds a tag and focuses the input; it does not toggle selection. There is no structured activity catalog, grouping, favorite/archive control or frequency/recency ordering in this flow. Limits currently include 20 tags, 40 characters per tag, 120 title characters and 20,000 note characters.

The calendar already has local-month navigation, mood marks, entry states, accessible day labels and daily-photo indicators. The timeline shows the newest 30 entries with note previews. The entry already lists same-day workout names and daily-photo thumbnails. Thus cross-domain context is **partial**, not wholly absent. A complete My Day joining food totals, check-ins/sleep, cardio, knee observations, journal and photo categories is missing from the reviewed journal flow.

Photos already have a categorized gallery and local compression/storage. JPEG blobs and thumbnails live in IndexedDB; metadata includes date, category and optional observations. Photos are not in localStorage. Daily photos join by date; deleting a journal removes daily-category photos for that date after the structured-data delete. Those two stores are not one atomic transaction; the UI reports partial cleanup failure. Full backup needs separate data and photo JSON files. These are real constraints for a richer journal, not reasons to replace the persistence model.

**Measurement issue:** `drained/Mệt` describes energy while the other labels mostly describe mood. Do not silently reinterpret old entries as a validated five-point mood scale or convert “Mệt” to “very unhappy.” Keep legacy values and labels; any new measurement semantics need a versioned definition and a clear historical boundary.

The core loop worth adapting is **capture → recognize the day → revisit → optionally reflect**. The user need not perform analysis every time they log. ## Mood model comparison and decision

| Model | Strength | Cost / ambiguity | Tracker decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Five ordered states | Fast, familiar, compact calendar; DailyBean and Daylio provide this general pattern [D4], [Y2] | Compresses a mixed day; ordinal labels do not guarantee equally spaced values | Keep initial capture small. Summaries use distributions; do not claim a precise mean wellness score. |
| Emoji / ink presentation | Easy recognition without reading every label | Color/face interpretations vary; icons alone are inaccessible | Use original marks plus Vietnamese text, focus state and explicit selected state. Emoji can be optional decoration, not the stored meaning. |
| Multiple feelings | “Pleased and worried” can coexist; Pixels tags and Daylio custom categories show richer vocabulary [P1], [Y2] | Feelings are not all ordered on good/bad; too many options slow capture | Optional small multi-select set, initially suggest at most two or three selections without imposing a clinical model. Never average feelings into mood. |
| Intensity | Distinguishes a slight feeling from a strong one | Repeated sliders add decisions; user interpretation drifts | Defer per-feeling intensity until demonstrated need; missing intensity remains null. |
| Energy | Can be low while mood is good; Bearable/Exist treat it separately [B1], [E1] | A second field is extra work | Optional low/medium/high energy behind “More context.” Version labels explicitly and keep old `drained` unchanged. |
| Stress | Useful personal context, independent from pleasure/energy [E1] | May become a compulsory daily survey | Optional later field with explained anchors and a skip control; never infer from mood or workout volume. |
| Fatigue | May be relevant to training observations | Overlaps energy and can be confused with soreness, sleepiness or distress | Avoid adding both fatigue and energy by default. If needed, define the exact question; retain knee/health observations in their own existing records. |
| Several moments per day | Pixels subpixels and Stoic morning/evening flows reflect variation [P1], [S2] | Changes one-entry/date invariants and summary weighting | Preserve one daily journal in the first upgrade. Optional future moment log needs stable IDs/timestamps, daily aggregation rules and an additive migration. |

## Original paper design proposal

Three approaches were considered: **A: strengthen the existing one-page journal**, **B: build a full habit/mood analytics system**, **C: add a guided AI journal**. Recommend A because it serves daily use with existing local records and preserves storage invariants. B adds catalog and missing-data complexity before the basic flow is proven. C adds remote-data decisions and uncertain interpretation without fixing capture/retrieval friction.

The proposed page has a date heading, the original five ink marks, a short row of favorite/recent activity chips, and a visible save action. A folded “More context” area holds optional feelings/energy, title and long note. Underneath is a photo strip and a **Recorded today** area assembled from existing records. Every summary opens its source screen for editing; there is no second workout or food form in the journal.

On narrow screens, one column follows that same order. Calendar days use text/shape indicators as well as color. A selected date opens a compact day panel; a photo-only day is visible even without a journal entry. A page-level local save status distinguishes pending, saved and failed. The first version can keep explicit Save; autosave should not be implied until conflict handling and draft persistence are designed.

Activity groups start small and optional. User-created IDs should remain stable across rename; archiving hides future choices while preserving old entry labels/meaning. Existing free tags must remain searchable, and a migration must not reclassify them without user choice. The simplest first release can improve tag toggling/recent ordering without introducing a catalog migration yet.

Photos stay in IndexedDB. Inline attach needs a deliberate orphan/partial-save rule because structured entry writes and photo writes are separate. A journal deletion should explain the already-existing daily-photo cascade; richer My Day links must not extend deletion to gym, food, progress photos, meals or workouts. Memory cards should be optional and hideable per entry because the app cannot know whether a past day is welcome.

## My Day and honest recaps

My Day is a derived read model keyed by the same local `YYYY-MM-DD`. Show the day's journal, completed workout count/names, recorded food totals, available weight/waist/sleep, cardio minutes, knee observations and categorized photos. A missing record reads **“Chưa ghi”**, not zero, skipped, rested or failed. A workout-free date is not automatically a rest day. Known nutrition totals are only totals of logged items; unknown recipe macros remain unknown. Multiple records must retain their provenance and open individually where needed.

A weekly journal card can say, for example, **“Đã ghi 4/7 ngày; 3 ngày chưa có nhật ký. Trong 4 ngày có mood: 2 Ổn, 1 Bình thường, 1 Chùng.”** This is an illustrative layout, not the user's data. If one of four entries has no mood, the mood denominator must be three, while journal coverage remains four. Activity counts should count distinct observed days, not every duplicate tag or every future intra-day check-in. Show period boundaries and use local dates consistently.

Weekly/monthly summaries should keep denominator differences visible: days with journal, days with mood, days with sleep and days with nutrition are separate coverages. A calendar blank cannot mean low mood. Do not give a “good week” score based on gym attendance, calorie compliance or positive-emotion frequency. Allow a user-selected highlight and a factual list of records without forced advice.

## Association rules before any correlation feature

Bearable's documentation specifies at least three days with and three days without a factor, measured outcomes on each, recommends a longer recording period, and explicitly discusses confounding [B1]. That is evidence of its product rule, **not** a validated sufficient sample size for Tracker. Exist advertises statistical relationships across manual/integrated data [E1], [E2]; this does not license causal claims.

For Tracker, first decide how to distinguish **present**, **explicitly absent** and **not observed**. With today's free tags, an unselected activity may simply be forgotten. Therefore do not label every date without “walk” as “no walking.” An association view can use a user-enabled tracking window or an explicit completion flag before treating reviewed-but-unselected factors as absent. Show the exact eligibility rule and let the user inspect included dates.

Any future comparison must display period, sample counts on each side, excluded/missing days, metric definition, and whether it is same-day or lagged. Report observations such as “Among the recorded days…” and state that they are associations. Avoid “caused,” “improved your health,” “best treatment” or automated workout/nutrition changes. Sleep-to-next-day joins require an explicit meaning for sleep date; do not choose silently.

Use at most a few user-selected comparisons initially. Multiple factors, tiny groups, weekday/weekend differences, reverse causation and self-selection can all produce misleading patterns. A chosen sample threshold is a conservative UX rule, not proof of significance. Suppress ranked “best/worst factors” when data is sparse. The existing mixed mood labels make distribution comparisons safer than arithmetic effect sizes until a new consistent scale has enough observations.

## Suggested sequence and acceptance evidence

1. **Capture/retrieve:** improve current tag buttons into reversible selections with recency/favorites; add local search and older-entry access. Verify selecting/unselecting, Vietnamese case handling, legacy tags, keyboard states and navigation without losing unsaved work.
2. **My Day + photos:** assemble read-only context, inline photo attachment and explicit partial-save errors. Verify empty days, photo-only dates, cross-midnight local dates, several workouts, unknown nutrition, failed photo writes and journal deletion boundaries.
3. **Descriptive recap + optional reflection:** show coverage, distribution and drill-through; add hideable memory cards and original local prompts. Verify independent denominators and no invented zero values.
4. **Catalog + richer observations if used:** stable IDs/archive semantics, optional feelings/energy, additive migration and backup compatibility. Keep historical meaning intact.
5. **Optional reminders/associations later:** test actual browser delivery capability and date semantics; correlations need an explicit observation contract first. No backend, sync, AI or medical interpretation is needed for the first three steps.

This report records opportunities, not test results. Existing project test counts belong to prior implementation reports and were not rerun for this documentation-only task.
